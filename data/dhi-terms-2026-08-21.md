# Redistribution Terms for Docker Hardened Images (DHI) Community Tier — Citable Memo

**Prepared:** 2026-08-21
**File:** `data/dhi-terms-2026-08-21.md`
**Scope:** Whether a personal open-source project may (1) build derived images using the `dhi.io/build` frontend, the `dhi.io/golang` builder, and `dhi.io/apk` packages on the free **DHI Community** tier; (2) publish those derived images publicly on GHCR under a personal namespace; (3) subject to what attribution/branding rules; and (4) whether `dhi.io/apk` may be used outside DHI images.

---

## TL;DR

- **Yes — building AND publicly redistributing DHI-derived images on the free Community tier is permitted, but the permission flows from the Apache-2.0 license on the images, NOT from the Docker Subscription Service Agreement (DSSA), whose §2.1 actually restricts redistribution.** The governing instrument for Community content is the catalog `LICENSE.txt`, which places DHI definitions/build config under Apache-2.0 and passes through upstream OSS licenses for the binaries and packages inside the images.
- **Apache-2.0 imposes real, enforceable conditions** (ship the license text, preserve NOTICE/copyright/attribution/trademark notices, mark modified files, no implied Docker endorsement), and **third-party packages inside the image remain under their own upstream licenses** — so a published image that *contains* DHI-origin binaries carries the combined obligations of everything inside it.
- **The public `dhi.io/apk/.../main` package repository is documented as usable inside non-DHI base images** (e.g., `FROM alpine:3.23`) with no paid tier and no authentication; only the authenticated `.../security` (Enterprise) and `.../els` (ELS) package repositories require a paid DHI Enterprise entitlement.

## Details

### Q1 — May Community-tier pulls of DHI content be used to build derived images?

**Answer: EXPLICITLY PERMITTED. Not limited to internal/evaluation use.**

Docker's product FAQ states the entire hardened catalog is "available to everyone – including for production uses," with "no paywalled image versions or usage restrictions." The Community column of the pricing table lists "Full catalog of open source images under Apache 2.0," "Drop-in adoption, no workflow changes," and "Built with Docker Hardened System Packages." Docker's launch blog is categorical: "here's the bottom line up front: every developer and every application can (and should!) use DHI **without restrictions**" (*Hardened Images for Everyone*).

Build-time use of all three artifacts is therefore permitted on the free tier:
- **`dhi.io/build` frontend** — the public, documented build mechanism; DHI YAML definitions begin `# syntax=dhi.io/build:2-alpine3.22` and are built with `docker buildx build` against catalog YAML.
- **`dhi.io/golang` builder** — the migration docs show `FROM dhi.io/golang:1.25-debian12-dev AS builder` as a standard build stage.
- **`dhi.io/apk/.../main` packages** — see Q4.

Authentication is required but no paid tier is. Docker's own developer post (Mohammad-Ali A'RÂBI, Dec 17, 2025) confirms: "The images are free to use, but you still need to authenticate before pulling them. Use your Docker Hub credentials to login. You can use your personal Docker Hub account and a personal access token (PAT) as the password. **No special subscription is required.**" Operationally this is `docker login dhi.io` with a Docker ID + PAT.

**Contract vs. documentation flag:** This permission rests on the Apache-2.0 license plus Docker's documentation/marketing, *not* on the DSSA. The DSSA governs paid subscriptions and is more restrictive (Q2). Because the Community catalog is released under Apache-2.0 and Docker's Terms of Use say Docker's terms "do not limit or expand any rights granted under those open source licenses," the Apache-2.0 grant is the operative authority for Community use.

### Q2 — May the resulting derived images be distributed publicly (GHCR, third-party pulls)?

**Answer: CONDITIONALLY PERMITTED — permitted under Apache-2.0 for the DHI-authored layers, subject to Apache-2.0's conditions AND the upstream licenses of every binary/package the image contains.**

The catalog `LICENSE.txt` (Copyright 2025 Docker Inc.) states: *"All Docker Hardened Image (DHI) definitions (Dockerfiles), patches and build scripts provided in this repository are licensed under the Apache License 2.0."* Apache-2.0 §2 grants a *"perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive, no-charge, royalty-free, irrevocable copyright license to reproduce, prepare Derivative Works of, publicly display, publicly perform, sublicense, and distribute the Work and such Derivative Works in Source or Object form."* Standalone public redistribution — pushing to a public GHCR namespace for third parties to pull — falls squarely within that grant.

**Critical distinction — tooling-only vs. DHI-origin content in the published layers:**
- **Tooling-only** (e.g., compiling a Go binary in `dhi.io/golang:...-dev`, then shipping that binary in a non-DHI runtime base): the published layers carry essentially no DHI license obligations, because none of the DHI-origin files are redistributed.
- **DHI-origin content in the layers** (e.g., a runtime stage using `FROM dhi.io/...`, or packages installed from `dhi.io/apk` that end up in the final image): you must satisfy Apache-2.0's redistribution conditions for the DHI-authored parts **and** the upstream licenses for the third-party components. `LICENSE.txt` is explicit: *"Third-party packages and binaries included in the DHI catalog remain subject to their original open source project or other publisher's upstream license terms. By using these images, you agree to the combined license terms of the included software. The list of licenses is available in the Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) at: https://dhi.io."*

**The contract-vs-license tension (must flag — NOT ADDRESSED by Docker):** DSSA §2.1 provides: *"Customers may redistribute Docker Images to third parties but solely when bundled with or incorporated into its own software products, and not on a standalone basis, and solely in accordance with the terms of this Agreement, including the restrictions in Section 4 herein."* If the DSSA governed the DHI Community images, standalone redistribution of a derived image would be prohibited. But the Community catalog is licensed under Apache-2.0 (which permits standalone redistribution), and the DSSA itself excludes registry/repository content from the "Docker Product Suite," stating that such "content available in a registry or repository … [is] instead subject to the corresponding third-party's license or terms." **No single Docker document expressly reconciles the DSSA §2.1 "not on a standalone basis" language with the Apache-2.0 grant for free-tier users.** The better-supported reading is that Apache-2.0 controls the images themselves; a cautious maintainer should nonetheless treat this residual ambiguity as unresolved (see Recommendations).

### Q3 — Attribution, notice, or trademark/branding requirements?

**Answer: CONDITIONALLY PERMITTED — Apache-2.0 §4 attribution conditions apply, plus an Apache-2.0 §6 trademark limitation and Docker's separate Trademark Guidelines.**

Apache-2.0 §4 requires anyone redistributing the Work or Derivative Works to: **(a)** *"give any other recipients of the Work or Derivative Works a copy of this License"*; **(b)** *"cause any modified files to carry prominent notices stating that You changed the files"*; **(c)** *"retain, in the Source form of any Derivative Works that You distribute, all copyright, patent, trademark, and attribution notices from the Source form of the Work"*; and **(d)** if a NOTICE text file is included, *"include a readable copy of the attribution notices contained within such NOTICE file."*

The catalog copyright line is *"Copyright 2025 Docker Inc."* The catalog repository ships `LICENSE.txt` but does **not** contain a separate `NOTICE` file at the repository root (its files are `LICENSE.txt`, `README.md`, `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `SECURITY.md`) — so the §4(d) NOTICE-reproduction duty attaches only if a given image ships its own NOTICE/attribution content. In practice, an image containing DHI-origin content should preserve the Apache-2.0 license text and any per-component attribution, and ship or point to the SBOM that enumerates each component's license.

**Trademark / endorsement:** Apache-2.0 §6 states the license *"does not grant permission to use the trade names, trademarks, service marks, or product names of the Licensor, except as required for reasonable and customary use in describing the origin of the Work and reproducing the content of the NOTICE file."* Docker also maintains separate Trademark Guidelines. Consequence: the "Docker Hardened Images" / "DHI" names and Docker logos may be used **descriptively** ("built from Docker Hardened Images") but not as part of the project's own branding and not in any way implying Docker endorsement or sponsorship.

### Q4 — Restrictions on using `dhi.io/apk` outside of DHI images?

**Answer: EXPLICITLY PERMITTED for the public `main` repository; EXPLICITLY PROHIBITED without entitlement for the enterprise/ELS repositories.**

Docker's documentation page *Use Hardened System Packages* (`docs.docker.com/dhi/how-to/hardened-packages/`, updated 2026-07-14) sets out a three-tier access model verbatim:

> "**DHI Community**: Includes hardened packages in base images. Can configure the public package repository to access the same packages in custom images.
> **DHI Select**: Includes all Community packages, plus access to additional compliance-specific packages (such as FIPS variants) and Docker-patched packages through the image customization UI.
> **DHI Enterprise**: Includes all Select packages, plus the ability to configure the enterprise package repository directly in your own images for full access to compliance and security-patched packages."

The same page gives a public-repository Dockerfile that **starts from a non-DHI base image** and adds the DHI apk repo without authentication:

```dockerfile
FROM alpine:3.23
RUN cd /etc/apk/keys && \
    wget https://dhi.io/keyring/dhi-apk@docker-0F81AD7700D99184.rsa.pub
RUN echo "https://dhi.io/apk/alpine/v3.23/main" > /etc/apk/repositories
RUN apk update && apk add libpng
```

So **adding `dhi.io/apk/.../main` to a non-DHI base image, and vendoring packages from it, is expressly documented and requires no paid tier and no authentication** (a parallel Debian example uses `https://dhi.io/deb/debian/main`). Supported Alpine versions include 3.23 and 3.24.

By contrast, the `docker-hardened-images/keyring` README states: *"The Enterprise and ELS package repositories require authentication and DHI entitlements."* Those are the `https://dhi.io/apk/alpine/vX/security` (Enterprise) and `.../els` (ELS) paths, gated by `docker dhi auth apk` and the `dhi-apk-sec` / `dhi-apk-els` signing keys — a paid DHI Enterprise entitlement. Docker's blog frames the "point apk/apt at Docker's hardened package repository" capability as an Enterprise feature; the granular docs clarify that framing refers to the authenticated `security`/`els` repos, while the `main` repo is public/Community.

**License carry-through:** packages pulled from `dhi.io/apk` remain subject to their upstream OSS licenses when vendored or redistributed (see Q2/Q3). Using the DHI-*patched* build of a package does not change the upstream license that governs it.

## Contract vs. Documentation — what actually binds

- **Binding contract terms:** the **Docker Subscription Service Agreement** (last updated May 29, 2026) governs paid subscriptions — §2.1 restricts standalone redistribution of "Docker Images"; §4.1(b) restricts "Personal Service Subscriptions" to individual, non-commercial developer/OSS/educational use. The **Docker Terms of Use** (effective June 24, 2026) defer to OSS licenses.
- **Binding license:** the **Apache License 2.0** in the catalog `LICENSE.txt` governs the DHI definitions/build config; **upstream OSS licenses** govern the binaries and packages.
- **Persuasive but non-binding:** `docs.docker.com` pages, the product-page FAQ, and Docker blog/press posts. These consistently say "no usage restrictions" / "use, share, and build on … without restrictions" — helpful evidence of intent, but not contract text.
- **No DHI-specific contract exists.** Docker publishes supplemental terms for some products (e.g., Docker AI Supplemental Terms) but **none specific to DHI**; there is no separate "DHI Terms of Service" or DHI addendum. The Docker Product Schedule PDF (referenced by the DSSA) is a product-*description* document, not a source of DHI redistribution terms, and could not be confirmed to name DHI Community verbatim.

## Registry / access-model verification

`dhi.io` is confirmed as the DHI registry domain (`docker login dhi.io`; `docker pull dhi.io/...`; catalog README: "This repository is the home of the Docker Hardened Images definition files … https://dhi.io"). DHI Community images are pulled directly from `dhi.io` at no cost; paid tiers (Select/Enterprise) instead **mirror** repositories into the customer's own Docker Hub namespace (`<org>/dhi-<repo>`). Timeline: Docker announced DHI on **May 19, 2025** as an enterprise-only commercial catalog; on **December 17, 2025** it made "its catalog of more than 1,000 Docker Hardened Images (DHI), built on the widely adopted open source distributions Debian and Alpine, free and fully open source under the Apache 2.0 license," opening it to "all 26 Million+ developers." A later "DHI Community" naming change was, per Docker, "just a name change" with "no licensing changes."

## Recommendations — what a cautious maintainer should do

**Stage 1 — Structure the build to minimize obligations (do this now):**
1. **Prefer tooling-only coupling.** Use `dhi.io/golang:...-dev` and the `dhi.io/build` frontend in build stages, then ship a runtime image that does **not** copy DHI-origin base layers where feasible (e.g., a statically-linked binary into `scratch`, a minimal non-DHI base, or your own base). This keeps most DHI license obligations out of the published layers and side-steps the DSSA-vs-Apache question almost entirely.
2. **Use only `dhi.io/apk/.../main`** (public/Community). Do **not** configure `.../security` or `.../els` unless you hold a DHI Enterprise entitlement.

**Stage 2 — If DHI-origin content ships in your published layers (the common case):**
3. Treat the image as **Apache-2.0 + upstream OSS**: include the Apache-2.0 LICENSE text, preserve all copyright/attribution/trademark notices, mark any modified files, and ship (or link) the SBOM that enumerates each component's license.
4. **Avoid brand confusion.** Describe origin factually ("built with Docker Hardened Images"); do not use Docker logos, the "DHI"/"Docker Hardened Images" marks as project branding, or any wording implying Docker endorsement or sponsorship.

**Stage 3 — De-risk the residual ambiguity:**
5. **Keep a dated evidence file.** Save `LICENSE.txt`, the *Use Hardened System Packages* doc, and the product FAQ as accessed on 2026-08-21, since documentation (not a contract) carries much of the weight here and Docker revises these pages frequently.
6. **Seek an explicit statement** from Docker addressing free-tier standalone redistribution (or a DHI-specific terms document). That would eliminate the DSSA §2.1-vs-Apache-2.0 tension. **Threshold that would change this advice:** if Docker published DHI-specific terms restricting Community redistribution, or amended the Apache-2.0 grant, public redistribution would need re-evaluation and might require a paid tier or removing DHI-origin content from published layers.

**Benchmarks that flip a conclusion:**
- Q4 flips to prohibited if you point at `dhi.io/apk/.../security` or `.../els` without an Enterprise entitlement.
- Q2 becomes contractually clean (no Apache-only reliance) only with a paid tier that bundles the image into your own product per DSSA §2.1 — but note §2.1's *own* "not on a standalone basis" clause would then restrict standalone image publishing, so for standalone public redistribution the **Apache-2.0 route is actually the more permissive one.**

## Caveats

- **The strongest permissions come from license + documentation, not a bespoke contract.** There is no DHI-specific ToS; the Apache-2.0 license does the heavy lifting, and Docker's "no restrictions" assurances are marketing/documentation, not contractual guarantees.
- **The DSSA §2.1 "not on a standalone basis" restriction and the Apache-2.0 standalone-redistribution grant are not reconciled in any single Docker document.** This memo takes the position that Apache-2.0 governs the Community images (supported by the DSSA's own exclusion of registry/repository content and Docker's Terms of Use deferring to OSS licenses), but flags the point as unresolved.
- **"Combined license terms" is the real compliance surface.** Zero-CVE hardening does not simplify licensing: every binary/package retains its upstream license, and a maintainer redistributing an image inherits all of them.
- **Docker revises these pages frequently** (the product page, DHI plans doc, DSSA, and Terms of Use all carry 2026 revision dates). Re-verify before relying on this memo beyond a few months.
- **The Docker Product Schedule PDF** could not be retrieved to confirm whether it names DHI Community; it is, per the DSSA, a product-description document rather than a source of redistribution terms.
